# Stage 04 — Open-Hole Tension, Isotropic Kirsch Verification

**Author.** J.C. Vaught
**Date.** 2026-04-29
**Stage.** 4 of 16. Verification (V) — closed-form check.
**Pipeline owner.** OpenRadioss `/IMPL/LINEAR` static, solid HEXA8, isotropic linear-elastic aluminum 6061-T6.

This stage verifies that the OpenRadioss linear-elastic kernel resolves a stress concentration around a circular hole in a finite-width tension strip to within 2 % of the Kirsch 1898 closed-form solution corrected for finite-width by Howland 1929. ASTM D5766 covers the *composite* open-hole tensile test. Stage 4 is the *isotropic* Kirsch verification; D5766 is referenced for context only, the validation target is the closed form, not the experimental composite OHT.

The pass criterion of master_plan.md §3 row 4 is "$K_t$ within 2 % at hole edge". This document fixes the geometry, mesh, material card, deck skeleton, reference solution, and the runner contract that delivers that number.

---

## 1. Overview

Geometry. Rectangular plate of length $L=200$ mm in the load direction, width $W=100$ mm transverse, thickness $t=5$ mm, with a single central circular hole of radius $a=5$ mm (diameter $D=10$ mm). The hole-to-width ratio is $D/W = 0.10$, equivalently $2a/W = 0.10$.

Material. Aluminum 6061-T6, treated as linear-elastic only; no plasticity, no damage. $E = 68.9$ GPa, $\nu = 0.33$, $\rho = 2700$ kg/m$^3$. Source: ASM Aerospace Specification Metals data sheet \cite{ASMAerospace6061T6}. The elastic regime is enforced by capping the applied far-field stress well below yield.

Solver. OpenRadioss implicit static, `/IMPL/LINEAR`, MUMPS-linked build (master plan §10 risk 2). Solid elements only (master plan §1 hard requirement).

Closed-form reference. Kirsch 1898 plane-stress polynomial for the stress field around a circular hole in an infinite plate under uniaxial tension \cite{Kirsch1898}, corrected for finite width by Howland's 1929 biharmonic series \cite{Howland1929}, with the standard tabulation of Peterson 1974 \cite{Peterson1974StressConcentration} and the third-edition Pilkey and Pilkey 2008 \cite{PilkeyPilkey2008PetersonStressConcentration}.

Plane-stress justification. Through-thickness $t=5$ mm vs in-plane characteristic length $a = 5$ mm gives $t/a = 1.0$, which is in the transition between plane stress and plane strain. The plate is thin compared to the in-plane extent ($t/L = 0.025$, $t/W = 0.05$) so plane stress is the governing 2D limit and is the convention of the Kirsch closed form. The runner records the through-thickness variation of $\sigma_{yy}$ and reports the *mid-plane* rim value as the comparison value, with the surface and interior values logged for completeness.

Why this exact stage matters in the pipeline. Master_plan.md §3 row 4 is the first stage where the FEM kernel must resolve a non-uniform stress field around a geometric feature. It is the gateway to Stage 6 (Tsai-Wu / Hashin / Puck on the same coupon) and Stage 16 (ballistic AP-PLY where stress concentrations around tow undulations and projectile contact dominate). If this Stage 4 number is wrong, every later stress-concentration result is suspect.

## 2. Geometry

Let the plate occupy

\begin{align}
\Omega &= \{(x,y,z) \in \mathbb{R}^3 : -W/2 \le x \le W/2,\ -L/2 \le y \le L/2,\ -t/2 \le z \le t/2\}\\
&\setminus \{(x,y,z) : x^2 + y^2 \le a^2\},
\end{align}

with $L=200$ mm, $W=100$ mm, $t=5$ mm, $a=5$ mm, $D=2a=10$ mm.

Load axis is $y$. Far-field uniaxial tensile stress $\sigma_\infty$ is generated by prescribing a uniform $u_y$ displacement on the upper short edge $y = +L/2$ while clamping $u_y$ on the lower short edge $y = -L/2$.

Polar coordinates $(r,\theta)$ are taken at the hole center with $\theta$ measured from the $+x$ axis. The Kirsch peak hoop stress is at $\theta = 0$ and $\theta = \pi$ on the rim ($r=a$), where $\sigma_{\theta\theta} = 3\sigma_\infty$ for the infinite plate. Equivalently, measured from the *load axis* (the $+y$ axis), the peak is at $\theta_{\mathrm{load}} = \pi/2$. The runner uses the $\theta$-from-$x$ convention internally and reports both.

Aspect ratios.

| Quantity | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| $L/W$ | 2 | sufficient for Saint-Venant decay between the loaded edges and the hole, see §5 |
| $D/W$ | 0.1 | finite-width regime, Howland correction applies |
| $L/D$ | 20 | confirms plate is long enough that end effects do not perturb the hole-edge stress |
| $t/D$ | 0.5 | sets the through-thickness mesh resolution requirement |
| $t/a$ | 1.0 | plane-stress / plane-strain transition; mid-plane sample used for comparison |

GMSH script. The mesh is built in `runner.py` via the `gmsh` Python API. The geometry is constructed as a 2D rectangle with a circular hole via `gmsh.model.occ.addRectangle` and `gmsh.model.occ.addDisk`, booleaned with `cut`, then extruded through the thickness with `gmsh.model.occ.extrude` to form HEXA8 brick elements (transfinite + recombine on each surface, then extruded with `numElements` ≥ 3 in $z$). See §3 for the meshing strategy.

## 3. Mesh

Element type. Solid HEXA8 (eight-node linear hex), `/PROP/TYPE14` general solid property, isoparametric standard integration (no reduced integration — for a coarse linear-elastic stress-concentration problem, full integration is acceptable; reduced integration with hourglass control is reserved for the explicit dynamic stages).

Topology. Butterfly / O-grid pattern around the hole (also known as a C-grid block with four radial blocks). The plate is decomposed in the $xy$-plane into

1. an annular ring of radius $r \in [a, r_1]$ around the hole, with $N_\theta = 64$ elements around the rim ($\Delta\theta = 5.625^\circ$, ≥ 10 elements per quadrant — far above the brief's "≥ 10 around the hole rim" floor), and $N_r = 12$ elements radially with geometric biasing toward $r=a$;
2. four outer blocks bounded by the ring and the rectangle edges, each transfinite-meshed and recombined to produce hexes that match the ring at its outer boundary.

The transition radius $r_1$ is set to $r_1 = 4a = 20$ mm. Beyond $r_1$ the Kirsch peak hoop stress has decayed to within ≈ 5 % of $\sigma_\infty$, so coarser elements are acceptable.

Through-thickness. Extrude with $N_z = 4$ elements in the thickness direction (above the brief's "≥ 3 elements through thickness" floor). All four layers are uniform; no biasing in $z$.

Element-size targets.

| Region | In-plane edge length | Source |
|---|---|---|
| At the hole rim $r=a$ | $\Delta s_{\mathrm{rim}} = 0.49$ mm (= $\pi a / N_\theta = \pi \cdot 5 / 64$) | $\theta$-discretization |
| Radial bias from rim outward | first radial layer 0.10 mm, geometric ratio 1.25 over 12 layers, last ring layer 1.43 mm | refinement at the stress concentration |
| Far field ($r > r_1$) | $\Delta s_{\mathrm{far}} \approx 2$ mm | acceptable for a uniform Saint-Venant region |
| Thickness | $\Delta z = t / N_z = 1.25$ mm | $N_z = 4$ |

Element count target. Roughly $N_\theta \cdot N_r \cdot N_z = 64 \cdot 12 \cdot 4 = 3072$ elements in the inner annulus plus $\sim 2000$ in the four outer blocks ≈ 5 k HEXA8 total. This fits comfortably inside an OpenRadioss `/IMPL/LINEAR` MUMPS solve on a workstation.

Mesh convergence. The runner sweeps $N_\theta \in \{32, 64, 128\}$ at fixed bias law and reports peak rim $\sigma_{yy}$ for each, the convergence rate, and the Richardson-extrapolated infinite-mesh value. The 2 % pass criterion is checked on the $N_\theta = 64$ baseline. If the Richardson estimate disagrees with the $N_\theta=64$ value by more than 2 %, the runner flags a refinement-required failure.

Quality. Aspect ratio at the rim $\le 2$, Jacobian $> 0.7$, no degenerate hexes. The butterfly pattern guarantees no degenerate elements at the four corners between the ring and the outer blocks (this is the failure mode of a naive radial mesh).

Mesh export route. GMSH writes Abaqus `.inp`. The `inp2rad` Python converter (master plan §4, audit row C) translates `.inp` to OpenRadioss `.rad`. The runner verifies that the converted deck contains the correct nodal IDs at the hole rim by re-reading the `.rad` and checking the rim-node coordinates against the GMSH model.

## 5. Boundary conditions and loading

Lower clamp. On $y = -L/2$, all three displacements are constrained: $u_x = u_y = u_z = 0$. This is implemented as a `/BCS` card on the node set "edge_y_minus".

Upper drive. On $y = +L/2$, $u_y$ is prescribed to $u_y^{\mathrm{drive}} = \varepsilon_\infty L = (\sigma_\infty / E) \cdot L = (50 \times 10^6 / 68.9 \times 10^9) \cdot 0.200 = 1.451 \times 10^{-4}$ m. Lateral displacements $u_x$ and $u_z$ are *free* on the upper edge. This produces a uniform far-field $\sigma_{yy} = \sigma_\infty = 50$ MPa once Saint-Venant has decayed (verified post-hoc by the runner).

Out-of-plane. $u_z$ is constrained to zero only on the *lower* edge (already covered by the clamp). The plate is otherwise free in $z$, allowing through-thickness Poisson contraction. This is the plane-stress-consistent BC for a free-surface plate.

Saint-Venant decay. The hole is at $y = 0$, halfway between the clamped edge ($y = -100$ mm) and the driven edge ($y = +100$ mm). The distance from the hole to either loaded edge is $100$ mm $= 10D = 20a$. Saint-Venant's principle gives stress decay over $\sim$ one width $W = 100$ mm; here the available decay distance equals one width on each side. The runner verifies that $\sigma_{yy}$ on the planes $y = \pm L/4 = \pm 50$ mm is uniform across $x$ to within 2 %.

Loading rate. `/IMPL/LINEAR` is a single-step linear static solve. The drive displacement is applied as a single `/FUNCT` ramp from 0 to 1 over a unit pseudo-time $T_{\mathrm{end}} = 1$ s scaled by $u_y^{\mathrm{drive}}$. No inertia, no damping. One step.

## 7. Reference solution

### 7.1 Kirsch 1898, infinite plate, plane stress

For an infinite isotropic plate of thickness $t$ loaded by remote uniaxial stress $\sigma_\infty$ along $\hat{y}$, with a circular traction-free hole of radius $a$ centered at the origin, the plane-stress solution is, in polar coordinates $(r,\theta)$ with $\theta$ measured from $\hat{x}$ \cite{Kirsch1898}\cite{PilkeyPilkey2008PetersonStressConcentration},

\begin{align}
\sigma_{rr}(r,\theta) &= \frac{\sigma_\infty}{2}\left(1 - \frac{a^2}{r^2}\right) - \frac{\sigma_\infty}{2}\left(1 - \frac{4 a^2}{r^2} + \frac{3 a^4}{r^4}\right)\cos 2\theta,\\
\sigma_{\theta\theta}(r,\theta) &= \frac{\sigma_\infty}{2}\left(1 + \frac{a^2}{r^2}\right) + \frac{\sigma_\infty}{2}\left(1 + \frac{3 a^4}{r^4}\right)\cos 2\theta,\\
\sigma_{r\theta}(r,\theta) &= \frac{\sigma_\infty}{2}\left(1 + \frac{2 a^2}{r^2} - \frac{3 a^4}{r^4}\right)\sin 2\theta.
\end{align}

(Sign convention: load applied along $\hat{y}$ gives $+\cos 2\theta$ for the part of $\sigma_{\theta\theta}$ that is *positive* at $\theta = 0$ and $\pi$, where the rim is perpendicular to the load; the formulas above are written for tension along $\hat{y}$, with $\theta$ measured from $\hat{x}$.)

On the hole rim $r=a$, the radial and shear components vanish identically, $\sigma_{rr}(a,\theta) = 0$ and $\sigma_{r\theta}(a,\theta) = 0$, and the hoop stress reduces to

\begin{align}
\sigma_{\theta\theta}(a,\theta) = \sigma_\infty\,(1 + 2\cos 2\theta).
\end{align}

The peak occurs at $\theta = 0$ and $\theta = \pi$ (the points on the $x$-axis, perpendicular to the load), where

\begin{align}
\sigma_{\theta\theta}^{\max} = 3\,\sigma_\infty,\qquad K_t^{\infty} = 3.
\end{align}

The minimum is at $\theta = \pi/2$ (the points on the $y$-axis, parallel to the load), $\sigma_{\theta\theta}(a,\pi/2) = -\sigma_\infty$ (compression).

In Cartesian components on the rim at $\theta = 0$ (i.e., the point $(a, 0, 0)$), the local hoop direction $\hat{\theta}$ coincides with $\hat{y}$, so $\sigma_{yy} = \sigma_{\theta\theta} = 3\sigma_\infty$. This is the FEM observable that `runner.py` extracts.

Net-section ligament decay (the line $y=0$, $x \in [a, W/2]$) follows from the same formulas at $\theta = 0$,

\begin{align}
\sigma_{yy}(x, 0) = \sigma_{\theta\theta}(r=x, \theta=0) = \frac{\sigma_\infty}{2}\left(2 + \frac{a^2}{x^2} + \frac{3 a^4}{x^4}\right),\qquad x \ge a.
\end{align}

This is the Kirsch polynomial decay from $3\sigma_\infty$ at $x=a$ to $\sigma_\infty$ at $x \to \infty$, used as a secondary check by the runner.

### 7.2 Howland 1929 finite-width correction

For a strip of finite width $W$ with a centered hole of diameter $D = 2a$, Howland 1929 \cite{Howland1929} solved the biharmonic equation by a series expansion in $2a/W$ and tabulated the gross-section stress concentration factor

\begin{align}
K_{tg} = \sigma_{\theta\theta}^{\max}/\sigma_\infty,
\end{align}

where $\sigma_\infty = P/(W t)$ is the gross-section far-field stress (load divided by gross area, ignoring the hole). The Howland tabulation, reproduced in Peterson 1974 \cite{Peterson1974StressConcentration} and Pilkey and Pilkey 2008 \cite{PilkeyPilkey2008PetersonStressConcentration} as Chart 4.1 (axial tension, infinite-length strip with central hole), gives

| $2a/W$ | $K_{tg}$ (Howland 1929) |
|---|---|
| 0.0 (Kirsch limit) | 3.000 |
| 0.1 (Stage 4 geometry) | **3.035** |
| 0.2 | 3.140 |
| 0.3 | 3.36 |
| 0.4 | 3.74 |
| 0.5 | 4.32 |

For $2a/W = 0.1$ the finite-width correction factor is $K_{tg}/K_t^\infty = 3.035 / 3.000 = 1.012$, i.e. only 1.2 % above the Kirsch infinite-plate value. The plate is, for the purposes of a 2 % pass criterion, *effectively infinite*, but the runner targets the Howland corrected value 3.035 to land safely inside the tolerance and to make the test discriminating against a wrong-direction error.

The associated net-section factor is $K_{tn} = K_{tg} (1 - D/W) = 3.035 \cdot 0.9 = 2.732$, used here only as a documentation cross-check; the FEM observable is gross-section because that is the well-defined far-field stress in the chosen BC setup.

### 7.3 Plane-stress vs plane-strain

The Kirsch closed form is plane stress. For a thick plate the plane-strain hoop stress at the rim is also $3\sigma_\infty$ (the plane-stress and plane-strain solutions of Kirsch coincide on the rim because $\sigma_{rr} = \sigma_{r\theta} = 0$ there, and the hoop stress is independent of through-thickness Poisson constraint at that point). The plane-stress / plane-strain difference shows up *off* the rim (in $\sigma_{zz}$, and in the $\sigma_{rr}$ / $\sigma_{\theta\theta}$ values at $r > a$), but not in the comparison value used here. The runner therefore needs no plane-stress / plane-strain switch and reports the mid-plane rim value as the canonical comparison.

### 7.4 Numerical reference value

For $\sigma_\infty = 50$ MPa, $K_{tg} = 3.035$, the target peak rim $\sigma_{yy}$ is

\begin{align}
\sigma_{yy}^{\mathrm{target}} = K_{tg} \cdot \sigma_\infty = 3.035 \cdot 50 \mathrm{\,MPa} = 151.75 \mathrm{\,MPa}.
\end{align}

The 2 % tolerance band is $[148.7, 154.8]$ MPa, equivalently $K_{tg} \in [2.974, 3.096]$.

The runner also compares against the Kirsch infinite-plate limit $\sigma_{yy}^{\mathrm{Kirsch}} = 3 \cdot 50 = 150.00$ MPa for completeness; agreement with this value to within 2 % is also expected and is reported as a secondary metric.

## 8. Post-processing and extraction protocol

The OpenRadioss `.h3d` output is converted to `.vtkhdf` by the Kitware `openradioss-to-vtkhdf` tool (master plan §4, audit row B). The runner reads the resulting VTKHDF in PyVista headless and performs the following extraction.

1. Identify rim nodes. Filter the mesh to the set of nodes with $|r - a| < \epsilon_r$ (where $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ and $\epsilon_r = 0.01a = 0.05$ mm), and $|z| < \epsilon_z$ ($\epsilon_z = 0.01 t = 0.05$ mm) for the mid-plane rim. This yields $N_\theta = 64$ nodes around the rim at the mid-plane.

2. Sample $\sigma_{yy}$ at the rim. OpenRadioss element output is at the integration points; the runner uses the cell-centered tensor field `STRESS` and interpolates to the rim nodes via PyVista's `cell_data_to_point_data`. A nearest-element fallback is implemented for nodes that lie exactly on the rim curve.

3. Compute $\sigma_{yy}^{\mathrm{peak}}$. The peak is reported as $\max_\theta \sigma_{yy}(\theta)$ at the rim, where $\theta$ is measured from $\hat{x}$. The expected peak is at $\theta = 0$ and $\theta = \pi$.

4. Verify $\sigma_\infty$. Sample $\sigma_{yy}$ on the planes $y = \pm L/4 = \pm 50$ mm (not on the loaded faces themselves, to skip Saint-Venant boundary-layer corruption), average over the cross section, and confirm the average is within 1 % of the input $\sigma_\infty = 50$ MPa. If not, the test fails on the BC sanity check before any Kirsch comparison is attempted.

5. Compute $K_{tg}^{\mathrm{FEM}} = \sigma_{yy}^{\mathrm{peak}} / \sigma_\infty^{\mathrm{measured}}$.

6. Polynomial-decay sanity check (secondary). Sample $\sigma_{yy}$ along the net-section ligament $y=0,\ x \in [a, W/2]$ at the mid-plane and compare to the Kirsch closed form (§7.1). The L2 relative error must be ≤ 3 % across the ligament (master plan §3 row 4 implicit, and the test_progression_literature.md row 4 secondary tolerance).

7. Through-thickness check. Sample $\sigma_{yy}$ at $\theta = 0$ rim node for all four $z$-layers. The variation across the thickness should be ≤ 5 %, demonstrating that the plate is in the plane-stress regime (master plan §1 + audit confirmation that solid HEXA8 with free top/bottom faces produces plane-stress at the rim).

8. Mesh convergence. Repeat the run for $N_\theta \in \{32, 64, 128\}$ and report Richardson-extrapolated $K_{tg}^{\mathrm{FEM}}(\infty)$. The extrapolated value must agree with the $N_\theta = 64$ value to within 0.5 %.

All extracted numbers are written to `results/stage04_metrics.csv` with columns

`[N_theta, sigma_inf_input_MPa, sigma_inf_measured_MPa, sigma_peak_MPa, Kt_FEM, Kt_target_Howland, Kt_target_Kirsch, error_pct_Howland, error_pct_Kirsch, ligament_L2_error_pct, thickness_variation_pct, status]`.

A Typst + CeTZ figure is generated showing (i) the rim hoop-stress angular distribution overlaid on $\sigma_\infty(1 + 2\cos 2\theta)$, and (ii) the ligament decay overlaid on Kirsch's polynomial. The figure is saved as `figures/stage04_kirsch_overlay.pdf` and `figures/stage04_kirsch_overlay.png`.

## 9. Pass criterion

Primary. $|K_{tg}^{\mathrm{FEM}} - 3.035| / 3.035 \le 0.02$ on the $N_\theta = 64$ baseline mesh. Equivalently, $\sigma_{yy}^{\mathrm{peak}} \in [148.7, 154.8]$ MPa.

Secondary (all must pass).
1. Far-field $\sigma_\infty^{\mathrm{measured}}$ within 1 % of input 50 MPa.
2. Ligament Kirsch-decay L2 error ≤ 3 %.
3. Through-thickness $\sigma_{yy}$ variation at the rim $\theta=0$ node ≤ 5 %.
4. Mesh-convergence Richardson extrapolation within 0.5 % of $N_\theta=64$ value.

Reported but not gating.
- Comparison against the Kirsch infinite-plate $K_t = 3.0$: error should be in the range 1–2 % (the finite-width correction makes a perfect match impossible on the chosen geometry, this is by design).
- $K_{tn}^{\mathrm{FEM}} = K_{tg}^{\mathrm{FEM}} \cdot (1 - D/W)$ logged as a documentation cross-check against Heywood-form net-section formulas \cite{Heywood1952DesigningByPhotoelasticity}.

Failure modes and what they mean.
- Peak stress *under* target by more than 2 % → mesh too coarse at the rim; refine $N_\theta$ and the radial bias.
- Peak stress *over* target by more than 2 % → likely a stress-recovery / extrapolation artifact; rerun with `cell_data_to_point_data` disabled and read element-centroid stresses directly.
- Ligament L2 error large but rim peak in band → mesh OK at the rim but coarse in the transition annulus; increase $N_r$ or extend $r_1$.
- Through-thickness variation > 5 % → the BCs are constraining $u_z$ somewhere (check that only the lower clamp constrains $u_z$ and the rest of the plate is free).
